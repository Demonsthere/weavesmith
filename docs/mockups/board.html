What you're looking at

Cards and picks are the two axes. Each note is the thread showing on the band's face at that card and pick, leaning / or \ with the twist — so the note field is the woven band.

↓ Band runs picks downward, cards across: the desktop layout. → Band transposes it — every card visible down the side, the pattern growing to the right as you scroll. That's the phone layout, and it's the default on a narrow screen.

Card colour encodes threading direction, not index, so it survives a 40-card band. Every fifth card is a landmark. Note faces stay wool-coloured; identity colour never touches them.

Editing

  • Click a note to flip it; drag to paint a run. Shift-click extends the selection without dragging.
  • Hover on a desktop: the note ghosts to what it would become and every cell below that would change gets a dashed outline — the ripple, before you commit.
  • Click a chip to open its card: threading, and a colour per hole. Long-press works on touch.
  • Adding a card opens that editor straight away, pre-filled from its neighbour.

Keyboard

  • ←↑↓→ move · Shift+arrows extend · PgUp/PgDn five picks · Home/End first/last card
  • Space flip · F/B forward/backward · 1–4 show hole A–D · S/Z threading · E edit card
  • Esc collapse · Ctrl/Cmd+Z undo · Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+Z redo

Arrow keys follow the band, not the screen: in → Band the axes swap with the layout.

Not final

Threading here is a plain chevron. Palette is dyed wool: madder, woad, weld, walnut, undyed — the presets in the card editor, with a picker for anything else.

Hole-position and lean conventions are a first pass; the real ones get pinned by tests against published bands.
